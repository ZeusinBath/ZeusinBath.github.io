Bath IFG held the first in this season's sessions of Scriptural Reasoning. Under the title "the fall, mercy and human responsibility" it took place 20 Oct at the University of Bath, hosted by the University's Coordinating Faith Leader Revd Ian Spence.

![University's Coordinating Faith Leader Revd Ian Spence erlcomes Imam Mohammed Gamal](/assets/images/ian-welcomes-imam.jpg)

*The University of Bath Coordinating Faith Leader Revd Ian Spence invites Imam Mohammed Gamal to introduce Scriptural Reasoning*

Imam Mohammed Gamal gave a broad introduction to the process of Scriptural Reasoning. Dating from the 1990s, it's an accessible practice which invites people to read each others' sacred texts with curiosity, honesty and respect. It's not a sermon, not a debate, not a search for consensus or attempt to diminish difference, but rather a process to help us better understand other, and to understand our own traditions better also. 

![Bath IFG members and guests during a close reading of different texts of the Fall in the Garden of Eden](/assets/images/ifg-members-reasoning.jpg)

*Bath IFG members and guests during a close reading of the story of the Garden of Eden.*

We discussed the Garden of Eden story from Christian, Jewish and Islamic texts under the title "The Fall, Mercy and Human Responsibility". The nuances are different and the story itself is surprising. We heard that Jewish and to some extent Islamic traditions do not share the Christian understanding of "fall". We found that the texts merit close scrutiny, both jointly and severally.
