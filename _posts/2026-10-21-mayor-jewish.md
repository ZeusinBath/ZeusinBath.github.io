## Mayor's theme: a Jewish perspective

*Bath IFG members were invited to respond from different faith perspectives to the Mayor's theme for 2026 "celebrating culture, community and curiosity". Iris Segall contributed a Jewish prespective:*


![Image Iris Segall in the Mayor's Parlour](/assets/images/iris-at-mayor.jpg)

There are many celebrations in the Jewish tradition. Whether we think of Jews who are more observant of the Jewish faith, or those who view themselves as secular Jews, celebrations are inherently a part of each family and community’s identity. There are many Jewish festivals throughout the year, many opportunities to appreciate what we have, think of the past and plan for the future. Celebrating in community is a building block in founding and preserving culture. Curiosity about how traditions started helps link past to present, answer questions, and educate while at the same time encouraging the creation of new traditions.

Celebrating Community, Culture, and Curiosity:

Community; or *Kehila* in Hebrew, provides a network of support. Celebrating festivals and day to day life with others, strengthens the sense of community. Sharing celebrations with others enhances bonds within communities. Repairing the world - *Tikkun Olam*, and doing acts of kindness - *Tzdakah*, such as charity and social justice have a special place within Jewish communities. They help form bonds within communities, among communities, and in the wider context of humanity.

Culture; following traditions and celebrating Jewish festivals and rites of passage are part of the Jewish culture. They remind people of their heritage and shared past with their community. They help bonding with their own families, as well as bridging the past with the present, thus promoting the continuity *M’dor L’Dor* – from generation to generation through retelling stories, listening to traditional music, and eating traditional food.

Curiosity; is and has always been encouraged in Judaism. In biblical times Abraham and Mosses among others questioned God’s words and actions. Over the centuries Jews have been encouraged to use critical thinking studying religious texts, reading in between the lines, analysing and interpreting the Torah. From that stems the spiritual thirst for knowledge, leading to research and a better understanding of humanity and science, encouraging advances in all fields, to create a better world for future generations.
